<p>So, you have beaten the "Lou's pixel art" puzzle, good job! But there's no time to celebrate.</p>

<p>Your <b>golden</b> robot is stuck inside a labyrinth full of winding paths and dead ends. Somewhere in this maze is the <b>purple</b> exit. But the journey isn't just about finding the right path; it's about <b>finding the fastest way through</b>.</p>

<p>The robot understands four instructions, each of which moves it one step:</p>
<ul>
  <li><code>u</code>: up</li>
  <li><code>l</code>: left</li>
  <li><code>d</code>: down</li>
  <li><code>r</code>: right</li>
</ul>

<p>The instructions for this smaller maze are <code>uuurddruur</code>. However, the larger labyrinth needs too many moves for the robot to process. <b>Speed and efficiency are the key to survival.</b></p>

<p>Fortunately, the robot remembers a trick from its programming: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run-length_encoding">run-length encoding</a>. This algorithm compresses instructions to make them shorter and faster. Here's how it works:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Do not modify single characters.</li>
  <li>For every sequence of the same consecutive letter, compress it by using the letter followed by the <b>number</b> of consecutive letters. For example, <code>uuur</code> becomes <code>u3r</code>.</li>
</ul>
